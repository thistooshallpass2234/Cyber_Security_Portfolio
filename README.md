# Cyber_Security_Portfolio
CTF Experience Reflection:
This is my first CTF experience with Cyber Skyline and it was relatively straight forward when it came to understanding the flow of each of the domains. Unlike other CTF events that I have participated in, this was simplistic in its interface and kept the entire event concise which is something that I rather enjoyed. 

In addition to its simplicity, this event had a specific start and end time without a mandatory break in between. This is something that I observed with NetWars, there is a 3 hour window each day for two days to accomplish as much as possible. This is great if you're geographically located with the host of the event. If you're not, then it can be kind of frustrating to have to do a bunch of hustling during your sleeping hours. 

Throughout the individual competition, I was a little skeptical with using AI when trying to solve many of the challenges. Something that really caught me off guard was the speed in which some of the other competitors were able to solve things. Then it occurred to me that they had incorporated AI agents to help with the accelerated solving of challenges.  Of course I leveraged AI to help with starting points, review some data here and there, and connect a couple of dots that I couldn't quite see yet. What I did notice was that some of the solution paths an AI model attempted to lead me down were inaccurate or constantly going in circles. This could be because I didn't give the AI total access to any of the challenge data, my prompts might have been less than specific to achieve a desired result, or it wasn't the right model for the job. In the end I found the missing pieces of information that was sitting on the screen in front of me before the AI could notice. 

That is all mentioned to say that experience does matter when it comes to data analysis. Knowing what to look for will significantly speed things up. At the same time, the utilization of AI will also increase speed and accuracy. A healthy blend of experience and AI is useful, so long as we remember that AI can do many things, but it still can't replace a human when it comes to making an informed decision using everything the human body has available that an AI will not.
